# DentDesk — Loom Script (word-for-word)

Runtime ≈ 6 minutes. Lines in **bold brackets** are what you do on screen; everything else is what you say.

---

## Before recording

1. Open 3 tabs: **website** · **dashboard** (logged in) · **Leads → Daniel Okafor** (keep ready)
2. Dashboard → Settings → **Load example data** → refresh
3. Close notifications, browser zoom 100%, Loom on "Screen + Camera"

---

## 0:00 — Hook

**[Website homepage on screen. Camera on you.]**

Hi, I'm Imtiaz. I want to show you something I built for dental clinics, and I'll keep it to about five minutes.

Here's the problem it solves. A patient messages a clinic at nine at night — "my tooth is killing me, can I come in tomorrow?" Nobody sees it until the front desk opens the next morning. By then, that patient has already booked somewhere else. Clinics lose bookings like this every single week, and they don't even know it.

DentDesk is an AI front desk. It sits behind the clinic's website and inbox, replies to every patient in under a minute, books the appointment, and gives the dentist one dashboard to see everything. Let me walk you through it — first as a patient, then as the clinic.

---

## 0:40 — The patient side

**[Scroll down to Contact Us.]**

This is the clinic's website. Nothing unusual — a normal contact form, the same one every clinic has.

**[Type as you speak: name, your email, service = Emergency / Pain, message: "My back tooth cracked last night and it really hurts. Can I come in tomorrow?"]**

I'll write in as a patient. "My back tooth cracked last night and it really hurts. Can I come in tomorrow?"

**[Click Send Message. Wait for the thank-you screen.]**

Now watch what happens the second I hit send.

**[Point at "See earliest openings".]**

The AI has already read the message. It understood that a cracked tooth with pain is an emergency, and instead of "thanks, we'll get back to you," it's offering the patient the earliest openings right now.

**[Click the button. Booking page opens. Point at the red urgent banner, then the time slots.]**

This is the patient's personal booking page. The visit type is already selected. These are the real open slots for the next seven days — anything already booked doesn't show up.

**[Click a slot, then Confirm.]**

I pick a time, confirm — and that's it. The appointment is created, a confirmation email goes out, and the clinic's calendar is updated. Ten seconds. No phone call, no back-and-forth, and zero staff time.

And if the patient doesn't book here — say they just close the page — the AI waits a few minutes and then emails them these openings itself. So nobody falls through the cracks.

## 1:50 — The clinic dashboard

**[Switch to the dashboard tab. Refresh.]**

Now let's switch sides. This is what the front desk sees every morning.

**[Point at the four cards, left to right.]**

At the top: appointments today, new leads this week, how many people are still waiting for a reply — with urgent ones flagged — and the conversion rate: how many inquiries actually turned into bookings.

**[Point at "Needs your action".]**

This section is the heart of it. It's a to-do list, sorted by priority. Urgent cases first. Then patients who replied and need an answer. Then drafts waiting for approval. Every item has one button: review and send. When this list is empty, the day is done.

**[Point at the chart and the two breakdowns.]**

Down here, lead volume over the last two weeks, where the leads come from — website or email — and what people are actually asking about. So the dentist knows at a glance if it's mostly cleanings, kids, Invisalign, or emergencies.

**[Point at the right column.]**

And today's schedule on the right.

## 2:50 — AI drafts, staff approve

**[Click Leads in the sidebar. Point at the "reply ready" badges.]**

Here are all the leads. See these badges — "draft ready for approval." That means the AI has already written a reply and is waiting for a human to okay it.

**[Open Daniel Okafor.]**

Let's take Daniel. He emailed the clinic — his crown came off, no pain, wants it looked at this week, and he's asking whether to bring the old crown.

**[Point at the purple AI summary.]**

The AI summarised it in one line, so staff don't have to read the whole email.

**[Point at the draft on the right.]**

And it wrote the reply: yes, bring the old crown, sometimes it can be re-cemented, otherwise we plan a replacement in the same visit — here are this week's openings, and here's his personal booking link.

Everything in that reply comes from the clinic's own settings — their hours, their services, their pricing notes. The AI never invents a price or a policy.

**[Click Approve & send. It appears in the conversation.]**

Staff read it, change a word if they want, and click approve and send. Thirty seconds, instead of writing from scratch. And every message, in and out, is recorded right here — with who sent it, the AI or a person, and which email it was replying to.

## 5:15 — Settings and how it connects

**[Click Settings. Scroll slowly.]**

Last thing — how much control the clinic has.

**[Point at the clinic facts.]**

Everything the AI is allowed to say lives here. Hours, services, insurance, pricing notes, tone. Change it here, and every reply changes with it.

**[Point at the three toggles.]**

Three switches decide how much to automate. Instant replies for emergencies. AI booking when a patient confirms a time. And auto-answers for simple questions like "do you take my insurance?" Turn them on as trust builds — everything else always waits for a human to approve.

**[Point at the Google card.]**

And it plugs into the clinic's existing Gmail and Google Calendar. Replies go out from the clinic's own email address, bookings land in the calendar they already use. No new tools for the staff to learn.
